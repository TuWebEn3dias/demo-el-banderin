Our Story

Founded on November 15, 1923, El Banderín began as "El Asturiano, Provisiones y Fiambrería", a general store opened by Don Justo Riesco, a Spanish immigrant from Asturias.

In the 1960s and 70s, his son Don Mario, a die-hard River Plate fan, started hanging football pennants in the shop. What began as personal decoration became a tradition that completely transformed the place.

By the 1970s, there were so many pennants that the name changed: "El Banderín" was born.

500+ Pennants — A Living Museum

Today the bar holds over 500 pennants from football clubs around the world: from Argentina's biggest teams to faraway clubs in Europe, Asia, and Africa. It is a true living museum of world football.

Notable Bar of Buenos Aires

Declared a "Bar Notable" by the Government of the City of Buenos Aires, a title that protects and celebrates the city's most emblematic bars for their history, architecture, and cultural value.

Today

Since 2019, Luis Sarni has kept the bodegón spirit alive. On Mondays, sports journalists from Radio Belgrano gather to discuss football. On Saturdays, there is live music.
